\subsection{Real-World Problems (Grade 8)}
\probset{8}
{\small Read the situation, pick a letter within 20 seconds, then check the Answer Key.\par}
\begin{gradebody}

\textbf{Term 1}

\mcq{After a lechon lunch, which organ releases stored bile into the small intestine to help digest the fat?}
{Pancreas}{Stomach}{Large intestine}{Gallbladder}
{D}{The liver makes bile and the gallbladder stores and releases it.}{A}

\mcq{A patient has a large part of the small intestine removed. What problem is most likely?}
{Cannot chew food}{Cannot store bile}{Cannot make saliva}{Poor absorption of nutrients}
{D}{The villi of the small intestine absorb most nutrients.}{A}

\mcq{Severe diarrhea leads to dehydration mainly because the large intestine cannot}
{make enzymes}{digest proteins}{produce bile}{absorb water}
{D}{The large intestine's main job is water absorption.}{E}

\mcq{A cut kangkong stem stands in red-dyed water. After a few hours the leaf veins turn red. Which tissue carried the dye?}
{Cork}{Epidermis}{Phloem}{Xylem}
{D}{Xylem carries water up from roots to leaves.}{E}

\mcq{Potted plants wilt faster on a hot, windy day mainly because of increased}
{absorption}{transpiration}{photosynthesis}{germination}
{B}{Heat and wind speed up water loss through the stomata.}{A}

\mcq{In peas, purple flowers (P) are dominant over white (p). A Pp plant is crossed with a pp plant. What percent of offspring will be white?}
{75\%}{50\%}{25\%}{0\%}
{B}{Pp $\times$ pp gives 1 Pp : 1 pp.}{A}

\mcq{A tall pea plant crossed with a short one gives \emph{only} tall offspring. The tall parent's genotype is most likely}
{TT}{Cannot be known}{tt}{Tt}
{A}{A Tt parent would produce some short (tt) offspring.}{D}

\mcq{Both parents are carriers (Aa) of albinism, a recessive trait. What is the chance their child has albinism?}
{1/2}{1/4}{3/4}{0}
{B}{Aa $\times$ Aa gives 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa.}{A}

\mcq{You find an organism growing on rotting wood. It is multicellular, has cell walls made of chitin, and absorbs its food. Its kingdom is}
{Protista}{Animalia}{Plantae}{Fungi}
{D}{Chitin cell walls and absorptive feeding are fungal traits.}{A}

\mcq{A microbe with no nucleus thrives in a near-boiling hot spring. It most likely belongs to}
{Archaea}{Fungi}{Eukarya}{Plantae}
{A}{Many archaea are extremophiles.}{A}

\mcq{Bats fly, yet they are classified as mammals, not birds, because they}
{lay eggs}{have wings}{eat insects}{have hair and nurse their young with milk}
{D}{Hair and mammary glands define the class Mammalia.}{E}

\mcq{The Philippine tarsier and humans belong to the same order because both have}
{wings}{tails}{forward-facing eyes and grasping hands}{scales}
{C}{These are primate traits.}{A}

\mcq{A plant is kept in a dark cabinet for a week, then a leaf is tested with iodine. It does \emph{not} turn blue-black because}
{the leaf absorbed the iodine}{iodine was too weak}{no photosynthesis occurred, so the stored starch was used up}{the plant made too much starch}
{C}{Without light there is no photosynthesis; the plant uses up its starch.}{A}

\mcq{In a sunlit aquarium, small bubbles form on the leaves of water plants. The bubbles are mostly}
{carbon dioxide}{water vapour}{nitrogen}{oxygen}
{D}{Oxygen is a product of photosynthesis.}{E}

\mcq{After sprinting in PE class, your legs ache. This is mainly due to}
{lactic acid from anaerobic respiration}{too much oxygen}{excess glucose}{\ce{CO2} build-up}
{A}{Muscles short of oxygen produce lactic acid.}{A}

\mcq{Burning forests and cutting mangroves both increase atmospheric \ce{CO2} because they}
{increase respiration of plants}{add oxygen}{add water vapour}{release carbon and reduce photosynthesis}
{D}{Burning releases carbon, and fewer plants remove less \ce{CO2}.}{E}

\mcq{In Rutherford's experiment, most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil. This shows that an atom}
{is a solid sphere}{has no charge}{is mostly empty space}{is made of electrons only}
{C}{Only a few particles bounced back from the tiny dense nucleus.}{A}

\mcq{An atom has 17 protons and 18 neutrons. Its mass number and identity are}
{35, chlorine}{17, chlorine}{18, argon}{35, bromine}
{A}{Mass number = 17 + 18; atomic number 17 is chlorine.}{A}

\mcq{A neutral aluminium atom (13 protons) loses 3 electrons. It now has}
{13 electrons, charge 0}{16 electrons, charge $-3$}{10 electrons, charge $+3$}{10 protons, charge $+3$}
{C}{Losing electrons leaves more protons than electrons; the protons don't change.}{D}

\textbf{Term 2}

\mcq{An electric current splits water into hydrogen and oxygen gas. This shows that water is}
{an element}{a mixture}{an atom}{a compound}
{D}{Compounds can be broken down only by chemical means.}{E}

\mcq{18-karat gold jewellery is gold melted together with copper and silver. It is}
{a mixture (alloy)}{an element}{a pure substance}{a compound}
{A}{The metals are not chemically bonded in a fixed ratio.}{A}

\mcq{An unknown element is in Group 1, Period 3. Which is it, and how does it behave?}
{Chlorine; halogen}{Sodium; very reactive metal}{Magnesium; noble gas}{Lithium; unreactive}
{B}{Group 1 metals have 1 valence electron and react readily.}{A}

\mcq{Party balloons are filled with helium instead of hydrogen mainly because helium}
{conducts electricity}{is a noble gas and won't burn}{is heavier}{is cheaper}
{B}{Hydrogen is lighter but flammable; helium is unreactive.}{A}

\mcq{Which element has properties most similar to chlorine, used to disinfect pools?}
{Sulfur}{Sodium}{Bromine}{Argon}
{C}{Elements in the same group (halogens) behave alike.}{A}

\mcq{Where oceanic and continental plates meet, the oceanic plate goes under. Why?}
{It is denser}{It is younger and lighter}{It is made of granite}{It is thicker}
{A}{Dense basaltic oceanic crust sinks beneath lighter continental crust.}{D}

\mcq{Mayon's steep, symmetrical cone is due to}
{alternating layers of thick lava and ash}{one huge lava flow}{wind erosion}{runny basaltic lava}
{A}{Composite volcanoes build up from many lava and pyroclastic layers.}{A}

\mcq{Pinatubo's 1991 eruption was extremely explosive because its magma was}
{very cold}{low in silica and runny}{high in silica, viscous, and gas-rich}{mostly water}
{C}{Viscous magma traps gas until it bursts violently.}{A}

\mcq{Hawaiian shield volcanoes have gentle slopes because their lava}
{cools instantly}{has low viscosity and flows far}{is mostly ash}{is very viscous}
{B}{Runny basaltic lava spreads into broad, gentle layers.}{A}

\mcq{Taal's 2020 eruption began with violent steam blasts when magma heated groundwater and lake water. This type is}
{effusive}{Hawaiian}{phreatic}{Strombolian}
{C}{Phreatic eruptions are driven by steam from heated water.}{A}

\mcq{Why does the Philippines have many active volcanoes and frequent earthquakes?}
{It has many mountains}{It lies along plate boundaries in the Pacific Ring of Fire}{It is surrounded by warm seas}{It is near the equator}
{B}{Subduction zones around the archipelago produce both.}{E}

\textbf{Term 3}

\mcq{A typhoon weakens after crossing Luzon but strengthens again over the West Philippine Sea. Why?}
{It regains heat and moisture from warm ocean water}{Mountains add energy}{The Moon pulls it}{The wind direction changes}
{A}{Warm seawater is a typhoon's fuel.}{A}

\mcq{PAGASA reports a tropical cyclone with \SI{190}{km/h} maximum sustained winds. It is classified as a}
{super typhoon}{typhoon}{tropical storm}{severe tropical storm}
{A}{\SI{185}{km/h} and above is a super typhoon (since 2022).}{A}

\mcq{During a typhoon, the wind suddenly becomes calm and the sky clears. What should residents do?}
{Go out and repair roofs}{Evacuate by boat}{Stay inside; strong winds will return from the opposite direction}{Turn off PAGASA alerts}
{C}{The eye is passing; the other side of the eyewall is coming.}{D}

\mcq{Fishermen notice extra-high high tides and extra-low low tides during the full moon. These are}
{tsunamis}{spring tides}{neap tides}{storm surges}
{B}{The Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned at full and new moon.}{E}

\mcq{Why is tidal power easier to predict than solar power?}
{Tides follow the Moon's regular cycle}{Tides happen only at night}{Tides never change}{Tides are stronger than sunlight}
{A}{Tides follow the predictable Earth--Moon--Sun motions; sunshine depends on clouds.}{D}

\mcq{A jeepney speeds up from rest to \SI{10}{\meter\per\second} in \SI{5}{s}. Its acceleration is}
{\SI{50}{\meter\per\second\squared}}{\SI{15}{\meter\per\second\squared}}{\SI{2}{\meter\per\second\squared}}{\SI{0.5}{\meter\per\second\squared}}
{C}{$a = \Delta v / t = 10/5$.}{E}

\mcq{A ball rolls down a ramp, speeding up steadily. Its distance--time graph is}
{a curve getting flatter}{a straight sloped line}{a curve getting steeper}{a horizontal line}
{C}{Increasing speed means an increasing slope on a d--t graph.}{D}

\mcq{A coconut falls from a \SI{10}{m} tree. Just before it hits the ground, its energy is mostly}
{chemical}{potential}{sound}{kinetic}
{D}{PE turns into KE as it falls.}{E}

\mcq{Two jeepneys move at the same speed, one empty and one full. Which has more kinetic energy?}
{They are equal}{The empty one}{Neither has KE}{The full one}
{D}{KE increases with mass at the same speed.}{A}

\mcq{A student does \SI{1500}{J} of work climbing stairs in \SI{5}{s}. Her power is}
{\SI{30}{W}}{\SI{300}{W}}{\SI{7500}{W}}{\SI{1505}{W}}
{B}{$P = W/t = 1500/5$.}{A}

\mcq{At Angat Dam, water flows down through turbines. The energy changes in order are}
{potential $\rightarrow$ kinetic $\rightarrow$ electrical}{kinetic $\rightarrow$ potential $\rightarrow$ electrical}{electrical $\rightarrow$ kinetic}{chemical $\rightarrow$ electrical}
{A}{Stored water has PE, which becomes KE that turns the generator.}{A}

\mcq{Vehicle side mirrors are convex because they}
{give a wider field of view}{form real images}{are cheaper}{magnify objects}
{A}{Convex mirrors spread light and show more of the road.}{E}

\mcq{A swimming pool looks shallower than it really is because of}
{reflection}{refraction}{absorption}{dispersion}
{B}{Light bends at the water's surface, so the bottom appears higher.}{A}

\mcq{A solar cooker uses a curved shiny mirror because a concave mirror}
{forms virtual images only}{converges light to a focus}{absorbs heat}{spreads light out}
{B}{Converging rays concentrate heat on the pot.}{A}

\mcq{Your friend reads books easily but cannot see the board clearly. Which lens corrects this?}
{No lens}{Convex}{Concave}{Bifocal convex}
{C}{Nearsightedness is corrected by a diverging (concave) lens.}{D}
