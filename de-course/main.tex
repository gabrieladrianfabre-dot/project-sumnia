\chapter*{How to Use This Course}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to Use This Course}

Each chapter opens with a \emph{diagnostic} of three prerequisite questions,
then motivates, defines, proves, and works examples by hand, with a short
reason beside every step. It ends with pitfalls, problems in four tiers
(\textbf{W} warm-up, \textbf{C} core, \textbf{H} hard, \textbf{P}
Putnam/olympiad-style), graded hints, and further reading.

\paragraph{No answers.} This book contains no answer key and no final answers.
Hard problems come with hints in three levels: the idea to try, the key setup,
and the first major step. Use the smallest level that unblocks you.

\paragraph{Notation.} Three derivative notations are used, each where it fits
best: Leibniz ($\der{y}{x}$, $\pder{u}{t}$) by default, Lagrange ($y'$,
$y''$) for compact algebra, and Newton ($\dot{x}$, $\ddot{x}$) for time
derivatives in mechanics and dynamics. Chapter~1 introduces all three; each
later switch is announced in a single line.
